# Medication Map Rules

Use these rules when adding or auditing medication-condition map sections.

## Source Priority

- Base medication classes and medication lists on the study guides first.
- Link medications to conditions primarily from course materials, including lecture slides, study guides, and course cases.
- Use outside pharmacology knowledge only as support when course materials are incomplete, and avoid adding unsupported detail when the course material is silent.

## Sidebar Content

- Descriptions, side effects, and considerations / contraindications should be based as much as possible on provided course materials.
- When course materials give partial information, preserve the course emphasis and avoid overfitting the sidebar to external reference-style detail.
- If a medication or condition needs supplemental clinical context, keep it consistent with the course framing and make it easy to revise when better course evidence is found.

## Slide Coverage

- Show all relevant slides for the selected medication or condition.
- Err on the side of showing too many slides, not too few.
- Prioritize contiguous slide series. If relevant matches occur on separated slides within a topic sequence, include the slides between them when they are part of the same deck/topic run.
- Exclude obvious title-only slides, low-information slides, and obvious duplicates when they do not add study value.

## Audit Checklist

- Confirm each medication class exists in the study guides or course slides.
- Confirm each medication belongs in the displayed class.
- Confirm each medication-condition connection is supported by course material.
- Confirm side effects and considerations match course emphasis.
- Confirm slide matching includes the full relevant teaching sequence, not just isolated keyword hits.
